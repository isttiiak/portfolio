## The problem

Online shopping runs on information asymmetry: sellers know far more about a product than buyers do, and the main signal buyers get, the star rating, is easy to game. A five-star product with 3 reviews outranks a 4.7-star product with 2,000. Raw averages ignore recency and throw away the richest signal available: what reviewers actually wrote.

My undergraduate thesis, **"A User-Centric Approach to Ensure Safe Browsing in E-commerce through Behavioral Economics"** (BRAC University, Fall 2024 to February 2025, supervised by Prof. Farida Chowdhury, PhD), tackled this with a Chrome extension built by a team of five. I took primary ownership of the rating system.

## Key features

- **Bayesian averaging**: every product's score is pulled toward a prior, with a strength that shrinks as reviews accumulate: a 5.0 from 3 reviews moves a lot, a 4.7 from 2,000 barely moves
- **Exponential time decay**: recent reviews weigh more, so a product that has declined can't coast on old praise
- **Review sentiment in two languages**: an OpenAI API pipeline extracts sentiment from English and Bangla reviews
- **Chrome extension delivery**: shows the adjusted rating directly on live e-commerce product pages, next to the platform's own

## Results

- Thesis graded **A (4.00)**
- The extension was evaluated in a **29-participant user study**, using chi-square tests and the System Usability Scale (SUS)

## Challenges

**No ground truth to check against.** There is no "correct" score for a product. Design decisions were grounded in the research literature, and the approach was benchmarked against established real-world rating systems.

**From paper to product.** Research code assumes clean inputs; a Chrome extension gets whatever the page serves. Running the rating system on incomplete review data from real product pages forced defensive handling the thesis alone never would have.

## What I learned

- **Bayesian shrinkage** as a practical tool, not just theory; priors are how you make small-sample data behave
- Evaluating with people, not only with numbers: a usability study tells you whether a tool helps, which no offline metric can
- Shipping research as a usable artifact makes it legible to people outside academia

The supervisor commended the originality and statistical rigour of the rating system contribution.
